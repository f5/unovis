# Chord Diagram

One month of data transfer between the six cloud regions of a SaaS product, both directions combined: each arc is a
region, sized by all of its traffic, and each ribbon is the traffic between two regions.

</LibraryComparison>

## What to look for

- **Who can draw it.** Three of the nine libraries have a chord diagram. AG Charts has one in its paid Enterprise
  edition; TanStack Charts, Recharts, Chart.js, Observable Plot and Vega-Lite have none.
- **The data.** Unovis and ECharts take the same list of nodes and links as their Sankey diagrams (ECharts links them
  by name). Nivo takes a square matrix, so the demo builds one from the links and fills both cells of every pair.
- **Colors and labels.** ECharts and Nivo give every region a palette color and paint its ribbons with it; Unovis
  draws the nodes in its main color and the ribbons in a pale blue, so its demo sets `nodeColor` and `linkColor`.
  Unovis curves the labels along the arcs and trims them to fit, ECharts writes them horizontally outside the ring,
  and Nivo along the outside of it.
- **Hover a region or a ribbon.** All three highlight its ribbons, fade the rest and show a tooltip. Nivo's ribbon
  tooltip lists the value at both ends.
- **Randomize the data.** The arcs and ribbons of all three animate to their new sizes.
- **Presets.** *Grouped by continent* (`nodeLevels`) adds a ring of continent arcs, which only Unovis can draw:
  ECharts and Nivo take a flat list of nodes. *Radial labels* (`nodeLabelAlignment`) is Nivo's `labelRotation`. ECharts'
  chord series ignores `label.rotate`, so its demo turns each label with a `labelLayout` callback and computes the arc
  angles from the data itself. *Thick arcs* (`nodeWidth`, `padAngle`) is a pair of
  options in all three.
